# Questions for the manufacturer

Written 2026-08-12. Everything here is a question only the maker can answer — most of it
either blocks a customer segment outright or sits behind a promise the site already makes.

Ordered by what it costs you to not know.

---

## 1 · Blocks the contract market entirely

**→ Do you hold BS 4790 and EN 13501 fire certification?** Without it no hotel, developer
or commercial project can specify the product at all. Ask for the certificates themselves,
and whether they are issued per batch, per product or per material.

Worth knowing before the call: **wool performs well on this test.** High moisture and
nitrogen content and a high oxygen index mean it chars and self-extinguishes rather than
melting and dripping. BS 4790 is a test method, not a bar wool struggles with. This is
likely a fee-and-paperwork exercise — but the specifier wants the certificate, not the
chemistry.

**→ Finished weight per m² at 6mm, 12mm and 20mm pile?** `/trade` already promises material
data including weight per m². If a designer requests the specification sheet today, that
figure has to exist. This is a live promise the site cannot currently keep.

**→ Any colour fastness or shedding test data?** Designers ask. Specifiers require it.

---

## 2 · The durability gap

The homepage lists five things a customer is covered for — approval before making,
exclusivity, design ownership, transit insurance, statutory rights. **None of them covers
the piece lasting.** That is the one risk a buyer carries alone on a non-refundable
commission, and it is the gap against competitors offering a multi-year quality guarantee.

You cannot offer a guarantee the maker will not stand behind, so this is the question that
decides whether one is possible at all.

**→ What do you warrant, and for how long?**

**→ What is covered specifically:** pile loss, latex backing delamination, colour fade,
edge binding failure, shedding beyond a normal settling period?

**→ If a piece fails in year three, who remakes it and who bears the cost?**

**→ Is the answer different for wall hangings**, which are under constant tension rather
than underfoot?

---

## 3 · Quality and who carries the risk

**→ ARS 1400 matching — what is the tolerance?** The site tells customers the dye reference
is exact and more reliable than a posted swatch. That claim needs to be true.

**→ Is there dye-lot variance between runs?** Relevant if a client ever commissions a second
piece to match a first.

**→ If the colour comes back wrong, who bears the remake?** You are taking a 50%
non-refundable deposit, so the answer determines your exposure, not theirs.

**→ Can you produce a strike-off or sample piece, at what cost and lead time?** The site
currently says no physical swatch. Worth knowing the real cost before that stays permanent.

---

## 4 · Physical limits behind published claims

**→ Is 15m × 10m a single piece or seamed?** The site advertises it as a maximum dimension.
If it is seamed, how visible is the join, and at what size does seaming begin?

**→ Realistic lead time at 6m², 20m² and 50m²** — and what actually extends it. The site
says about four weeks from sign-off for a straightforward piece.

**→ Monthly capacity, and what happens when two large commissions land in the same week?**

**→ Wall hangings at the ~18m² maximum — what does one weigh?** The site tells customers to
confirm their wall and fixings will carry it, and promises to advise on weight at enquiry.
That advice needs a number behind it.

---

## 5 · Decides whether process content is ever possible

**→ Are you willing to be named, credited, or filmed?**

This one question decides a whole marketing channel. `COMPLIANCE.md` forbids publishing
manufacturing, workshop or supplier detail, so process content — tufting, shearing, carving,
backing — is locked until this is answered. Three outcomes:

- **Willing to be filmed, and you are happy to publish the relationship** → process content
  unlocks, with a written licence and credit.
- **Not willing to be named** → it stays locked, permanently. The design-led approach is
  built to stand alone without it.
- **Anything in between** → treat as locked until it is in writing.

**Never resolve this with stock or third-party tufting footage.** Presenting another
workshop's process as your own carries the same strict liability under the DMCC Act 2024 as
a fabricated review.

---

## What to do with the answers

The fire certificate and the weight figures go straight into the `/trade` specification
sheet. The guarantee answer decides whether the site can add a sixth assurance — the only
one that would cover the piece itself. The naming answer unlocks or permanently closes
process content.
