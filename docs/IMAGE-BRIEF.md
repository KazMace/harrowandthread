# Image brief — regenerating the site imagery

Written 2026-09-13 after research. Replaces the one-prompt approach in `IMAGE-PROMPT-mansion.md`
for everything except the hero. Read `PHOTO-SLOTS.md` for the file names and sizes.

## What was researched

- The mill's own answers (`MANUFACTURER-ANSWERS.md`): what the product physically is.
- The client's imagery guide (`large_scale_luxury_rugs_mansion_guide.md`), read in full.
- The client's own reference folder: about 70 images. Rooms range from a Georgian townhouse to a
  brutalist concrete house, a Mediterranean farmhouse, a coastal bedroom, a conservatory, a
  penthouse and a child's room. Palettes are muted. Camera is eye-level or slightly raised. Rugs are
  shown whole. Fibre close-ups (wool, silk, bamboo) are part of the set.
- Outside: hand-tufted vs hand-knotted construction and edge finishes; how the serious tufting
  houses describe their process (cartoon, tufting, over-tufting, hand carving, shearing); how heavy
  textiles are mounted on walls; hand-tufted wall-to-wall as a real product for palaces and yachts.

## What is actually sold, and what the pictures must show

| Fact | Where it comes from | What it means for the picture |
|---|---|---|
| Standard piece is hand-tufted 100% wool, latex and cotton backing | Mill Q45 | Edges are **bound or overlocked**. No fringe, no tassels. Pile is dense, even, velvet-like. |
| Hand-knotted offered at higher qualities | Price list rows 6–14 | A **heritage or medallion** design may be knotted. Only then is a short, even fringe on the two ends correct. |
| Carving, multi-level pile, loop-and-cut all possible | Mill Q11 | Texture is a legitimate design tool. Sculpted surfaces are honest. |
| Colour matched to ARS 1,400 | Site copy | Colours are dyed, saturated, even; not "distressed" or faded. |
| Max single piece 6 × 10m without a seam | Mill Q51 | Very large rugs are seamed. A seam is invisible from a room shot, so nothing changes visually, but do not caption a piece "one piece" above that size. |
| Wall hanging hangs from **wide loops along the top edge** | Client rule, 2026-09-13 | **The first rule for any wall hanging picture.** A row of wool loops about 20cm wide, one every 30–40cm across the full width (a 2m piece has 5–6, a 4m piece 10–11), every loop threaded onto the pole. The weight is carried across the whole width so it hangs flat. Never two strings, never corner ties, never a hidden sleeve. (The mill's standard is a rod pocket; loops are what H&T specifies.) |
| Wall-to-wall hand-tufted carpet, supply only, fitter trims | Site copy; industry precedent | Fitted floor, meeting skirting cleanly, one continuous surface. **Not on stairs.** |
| No bathrooms | Care page, client rule | Never. |
| Mill never named; no workshop or country detail | Compliance | No artisan, loom, factory or "where it is made" scenes. Fibre and finished-piece close-ups only. |

## Design directions (from the client's guide, §6–9)

Use these four, and stop using bold primary-colour poster graphics on marble.

1. **Warm minimalism, organic texture.** Ivory, sand, taupe, mushroom, stone, mineral green, muted
   brown, charcoal, soft clay. Interest from pile variation, high-low, loop-and-cut, tonal shifts.
2. **Painterly abstraction.** Fluid fields, atmospheric gradients, restrained colour relationships.
   Reads as a painting on the floor. Best in double-height and gallery-like rooms.
3. **Neo-deco geometry.** Restrained geometric order, symmetry, negative space, controlled contrast.
4. **Tonal heritage.** A medallion or all-over classical design in a quiet palette. This is the
   hand-knotted look; fringe allowed here and only here.

Plain single-colour pieces belong in 1: the interest is the pile and the light on it.

## Camera, light, finish (from the client's own references)

- Eye level or a little above. Never a drone-style top-down. Never fisheye.
- The whole piece in frame with floor visible beyond every edge. Furniture sitting on the rug.
- Natural daylight, one direction, soft. Warm lamp light allowed in evening rooms. No HDR glow.
- Matte, calm, slightly warm. Editorial interiors photography, not a render.
- One room per picture. The same room never appears twice on the site.
- The piece is the reason the room is photographed, but the room is real: a lived-in scale, not
  a palace in every frame. One grand hall in the whole set (the hero).

## Labels and metadata

- On-page label: one small line, "Plates are design visualisations". Not a header.
- Every file carries the IPTC `DigitalSourceType = trainedAlgorithmicMedia` tag (already in place
  for existing files; the generator adds it on export).
- No text, logos or brand names in any image.

## Process

One image at a time. Shown before the next is made. Cost is about 4p per image; a full set with
one retry each is under £2.
