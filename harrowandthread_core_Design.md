•   Write for Scanners: Users do not read; they scan headlines and images. Use bolded key points and short paragraphs. Make critical information unmissable.
•   The Blink Test: First impressions happen in under half a second. A professional look triggers the "halo effect," signalling high quality; a cheap look triggers scepticism.
•   Prioritize Speed: If a site takes over 3 seconds to load, you lose nearly half your visitors. Eliminate bloated plugins, uncompressed images, and unnecessary animations.
•   Avoid Over-Engineering: Keep animations functional (e.g., hover states, natural fades). If an animation distracts from the offer or slows the site, it is a failure.
•   Focus on Clarity: A beautiful site is a failure if users cannot identify what you do or how to buy within seconds. Design must serve the message, not the other way around.
Content & Strategy
•   Ditch Stock Imagery: Users recognize and distrust generic stock photos. Use  ai photos to provide a genuine human connection—this is a major trust signal. 
•   Cut Your Copy: If a sentence doesn’t add value, remove it. Aim for maximum brevity to respect the user’s time.
•   Be Bold with Pricing: Either show your prices or provide a "starting from" figure. This filters out bad leads and attracts those who are the right fit.  We start at around £6k for a basic design.
•   Differentiate to Avoid Commodity Status: If your site looks exactly like your competition, you will be forced to compete on price. Use your site to showcase your unique personality and value.
•   Consolidate Pages: Fewer, high-impact pages are better than a dozen mediocre ones. Too many pages create confusion and increase the chance of a visitor leaving.
Mindset & Process
•   Design for the Customer, Not Yourself: Stop obsessing over your personal preferences or internal brand documents. Use data to determine what works and focus entirely on solving the visitor's problem.
•   The About Page is for Them: Do not write a resume. Use this page to prove you understand the visitor's problem and have a track record of solving it. Use metrics (revenue generated, clients served) to build trust.
•   Repel the Wrong People: A great website is a filter. Be clear about who you are and who you serve so you spend your time only with the right clients.
•   Launch is the Start: A website is a living tool that requires ongoing data gathering, testing, and optimization.
•   Sell Results, Not Features: Customers don't want a "website"; they want leads, sales, and authority. Focus your copy and design on the transformation your business provides.
To make a website feel premium, apply these three core psychological principles:
1. The Halo Effect (First Impressions)
Users form an opinion in about 50 milliseconds. If the initial view (the "hero" section) looks professional, visitors subconsciously assume your entire company and products are high quality.
•   Action: Obsess over the top half of your homepage. Define the single feeling you want to evoke (e.g., confidence, calm, excitement) and engineer your hero section to trigger that specific impression immediately.
2. Cognitive Load & Cognitive Fluency
Brains are "lazy" and prefer to conserve energy. High-clutter designs force the brain to work harder (high cognitive load), which feels stressful and unprofessional. Simple, intuitive designs create "cognitive fluency," which users interpret as trustworthy and high quality.
•   Action: Declare war on clutter. Increase white space, simplify navigation, and establish a clear visual hierarchy where each section has one primary goal. What seems simple to you may still be complex to a new visitor.
3. Micro-Interactions (The Peak-End Rule)
People remember experiences based on their most intense moments ("peaks") and how they ended. Small, thoughtful details—like hover effects, smooth scrolling, or confirmation feedback—create "peaks" of positive emotion that make a site feel alive.
•   Action: Look for every opportunity to add small moments of delight. Use animations and feedback loops to show you care about the user experience, signalling craftsmanship and attention to detail.

 Materials & Construction

 What materials do you use?
We work primarily with high-quality wool, which offers durability, softness, and natural stain resistance. We also offer silk blends, bamboo silk, and traditional flatweave materials. Your material choice affects price, texture, and appearance.

 What's the difference between pile and flatweave?
Pile rugs have a plush, raised surface created by loops of yarn cut or left intact. They offer depth, warmth, and a luxurious feel underfoot. Flatweave rugs are woven flat with no pile—lightweight, reversible, and excellent for high-traffic areas or under furniture.

 How do I choose the right pile height?
Low pile (6mm) is sleek and easy to clean, ideal for high-traffic areas. Medium pile (12mm) offers a balance of comfort and practicality. High pile (20mm) provides maximum softness and luxury but requires more care. Consider your room's traffic and usage when choosing.

 Rug Care & Maintenance Guide

 Regular Maintenance

 Vacuum your rug regularly, ideally once a week, using a vacuum without a beater bar for wool rugs.
 Rotate your rug 180 degrees every 6 months to ensure even wear and sun fading.
 Place a rug pad underneath to prevent slipping and add extra cushioning.
 Keep your rug away from direct sunlight to prevent colour fading over time.

---

 Spill Management

 Act immediately on spills—blot liquids with a clean, dry white cloth or paper towel.
 Never rub a spill, as this can push it deeper into the fibres and spread the stain.
 For solid spills, gently scrape up the material before blotting.
 Work from the outside of the stain inward to prevent it spreading.
 If needed, use a small amount of lukewarm water and mild soap, but always test on an inconspicuous area first.
---

 Professional Cleaning

 Have your rug professionally cleaned every 2-3 years, or more frequently for high-traffic areas.
 Choose a cleaner specialising in handwoven or antique rugs, as standard carpet cleaners may damage delicate fibres.
 Always inform the cleaner of your rug's specific materials and construction.
 Avoid steam cleaning or wet washing unless recommended by a specialist.

---

 How do I care for my rug?

Care instructions vary by material. Wool rugs benefit from regular vacuuming and occasional professional cleaning. Spills should be blotted immediately—never rub. We provide detailed care instructions specific to your rug's materials upon delivery.

 Can I use my rug in a bathroom or outdoor area?
While some materials are more moisture-resistant than others, we generally recommend indoor use for most rugs. If you're considering a bathroom application, please discuss this with us so we can recommend appropriate materials.

Remove shoes when walking on rugs to minimise dirt and wear.
 Trim any loose fibres or snags carefully—do not pull them.
 Use castor cups or furniture pads under heavy furniture to prevent crushing the pile.
 Treat your rug with a professional fabric protector upon delivery for added resistance to spills.
